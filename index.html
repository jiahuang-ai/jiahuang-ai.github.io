Jia Huang

Video world models · Video generation · Preference & reward post-training

I am an M.S. student in Applied Analytics at Columbia University. I study how to make generative video models usable as world models: keeping them physically consistent and improving them through preference and reward post-training. My work covers simulator-grounded video data, reward-guided post-training of video world models, and region-aware preference optimization for video inpainting.

I currently work with Prof. Chenliang Xu at the University of Rochester on the VIGIL project (ARPA-H PARADIGM), and previously worked with Prof. Hao Tang at Peking University on physically consistent video world models. PVI-DPO: Perceptual Video Inpainting via Informative-Safe Preference Mining and Region-Aware Direct Preference Optimization

Jia Huang, Hongyou Zhou, Qi Huang, Hongbin Liu, Zhengqi Sun, Lumin Xu

Under review at AAAI 2027 (advanced to Phase 2)

Annotator-free preference mining for video inpainting: the clean video is the winner and a mid-difficulty model rollout, composited only inside the mask, is the loser. A region-aware, winner-safe DPO objective weights the masked region, its boundary, and visible context separately, improving the DiffuEraser backbone on every reported metric on DAVIS and YouTube-VOS.

SVP-Rerank: Difficulty-Conditioned Speech–Visual–Procedural Evidence Reranking for Medical Instructional Video Retrieval

Peng Zhang, Hongyou Zhou, Jia Huang

NLPCC 2026 Shared Tasks & Evaluation (accepted) · 1st of 10 teams, Track 2

Builds an offline evidence bank of ASR transcripts, VLM-extracted visual fields, and procedure-aware step units from raw videos, then routes simple and complex queries to different evidence and reranks with a lightweight query-normalized reranker.

Other manuscripts (causal inference)

Robust Propagation-Integrated Inference for Observational Studies with Network Interference

Pengyun Wang, Qi Huang, Jia Huang, Yutong Zhou

Under review at ICLR 2027

Under network interference, treatment-propensity errors propagate through the network into exposure probabilities. PIN builds observable propagation directions via cross-fitting and uses them for both point estimation and a propagation-integrated standard error, keeping 97–98% coverage while reducing absolute bias by 32–78% over the state-of-the-art baseline.

Simultaneous Inference for Proximal Causal Learning with Weak Proxies

Pengyun Wang, Qi Huang, Jia Huang, Yutong Zhou

Under review at ICLR 2027

Simultaneous confidence bands for counterfactual distributions and quantile treatment effects when proxies are weak. A sharper population-bias bound replaces the Cauchy–Schwarz bound (which overestimates bias ~50×), yielding quantile-effect bands with 99.8–100% coverage at only ~20% wider than oracle bands.
